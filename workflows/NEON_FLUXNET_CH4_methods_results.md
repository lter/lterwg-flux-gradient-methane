# NEON-FLUXNET CH4 Flux Comparison Methods And Results

## Methods

We used `NEON.FLUXNET.CH4FluxComparison.R` to compare ERA5-gapfilled NEON tower-gradient CH4 budgets with published ecosystem-scale, soil-chamber, and process-model CH4 flux benchmarks. NEON values were taken from the ERA5 annual-budget products generated by `NEON.ERA5.HalfHourlyGapfill.R`: `OUTPUT/NEON_ERA5_gapfilled_annual_budget_by_year.csv` and `OUTPUT/NEON_ERA5_gapfilled_mean_annual_budget.csv`. These annual budgets retained observed half-hourly gradient/tower fluxes where available and filled missing half-hours with predictions from the ERA5-driven generalized additive model. Site-year annual budgets were expressed as g C m-2 yr-1 and converted to daily rates as:

`daily CH4 flux = annual budget * 1000 / 365`

so all NEON and reference values were compared in mg C m-2 d-1. Positive values indicate net CH4 emission to the atmosphere, and negative values indicate net CH4 uptake.

For each NEON site, annual ERA5-gapfilled budgets were summarized as the median daily equivalent across available site-years. Site behavior classes were taken from the ERA5 annual behavior classification when available, with gradient and total-flux behavior classes from `OUTPUT/30min_site_behavior.csv` used as fallbacks. Sites were classified as consistent sources when at least 75% of site-years had positive annual budgets, consistent sinks when no more than 25% of site-years had positive annual budgets, and fluctuating otherwise. Site-level medians and interquartile ranges were summarized by ERA5 annual behavior class and by NEON ecosystem type. NEON ecosystem types were harmonized with the reference ecosystem groups; in particular, NEON grassland sites were grouped with the Grassland/Savanna reference category.

Published FLUXNET-CH4 ecosystem-class annual means and standard deviations were taken from Delwiche et al. (2021). Table 1 values were used for salt marsh, wet tundra, upland, drained, bog, mangrove, rice, fen, swamp, lake, and marsh classes, and the urban value was taken from Delwiche et al. (2021), Sect. 3.1.3. These annual rates were converted from g C m-2 yr-1 to mg C m-2 d-1 by multiplying by 1000 and dividing by 365. FLUXNET classes were assigned to broader plotting groups including Wetland, Lake, Cropland, Tundra, Urban, and Upland/Desert; the FLUXNET Upland and Drained classes were grouped as Upland/Desert.

We also included two soil-focused benchmark sets. First, process-based upland soil CH4 uptake rates from MeMo v1.0 (Murguia-Flores et al. 2018, Table 9) were converted from mg CH4 m-2 yr-1 to mg C m-2 d-1 by multiplying by 12/16, dividing by 365, and assigning a negative sign so that uptake followed the NEON sign convention. Second, soil-chamber literature benchmarks were compiled for wetland emissions, forest uptake, desert uptake, grassland/aerobic soil uptake, and cropland fluxes. Chamber values reported as CH4 mass were converted to C mass by multiplying by 12/16, and all chamber values were reported as mg C m-2 d-1.

The comparison figure aligned reference and NEON estimates by broad ecosystem group. For each reference ecosystem/source combination, multiple estimates were consolidated to a single plotted median with an error bar spanning the available range or reported uncertainty. NEON points show site-level ERA5-gapfilled daily equivalents, while larger NEON symbols show ecosystem-group summaries with interquartile ranges. Because the ERA5 annual products contained low observed half-hourly coverage, the NEON annual values were interpreted as model-gapfilled annual estimates rather than purely observed annual budgets. In the current input data set, observed half-hourly coverage had a median of 2.1%, a mean of 5.0%, and a maximum of 31.5% across site-years.

## Results

The ERA5-gapfilled NEON comparison included 39 sites and 150 site-years from 2021-2024. The site set included 22 forests, 9 grasslands/savannas, 5 shrublands, 2 croplands, and 1 wetland. ERA5 annual behavior classified 32 sites as consistent sources, 6 as consistent sinks, and 1 as fluctuating. Across all sites, median ERA5-gapfilled daily-equivalent CH4 flux was 0.932 mg C m-2 d-1, with an interquartile range of 0.430 to 1.45 mg C m-2 d-1. Site medians ranged from -0.732 to 4.27 mg C m-2 d-1.

Consistent source sites had positive but low-magnitude daily-equivalent fluxes relative to wetland-dominated FLUXNET benchmarks. Their median site-level flux was 1.16 mg C m-2 d-1, with an interquartile range of 0.665 to 1.56 mg C m-2 d-1 and a maximum site median of 4.27 mg C m-2 d-1. The single fluctuating site had a median daily-equivalent flux of 0.027 mg C m-2 d-1. Consistent sink sites had a median of -0.441 mg C m-2 d-1, with an interquartile range of -0.554 to -0.308 mg C m-2 d-1 and site medians ranging from -0.732 to -0.094 mg C m-2 d-1.

Published FLUXNET-CH4 ecosystem-class means were much larger than the NEON ERA5-gapfilled source-class median for high-emission ecosystem types. Converted FLUXNET daily rates were 127 mg C m-2 d-1 for the urban site, 112 mg C m-2 d-1 for marshes, 77.3 mg C m-2 d-1 for lakes, 72.3 mg C m-2 d-1 for swamps, 56.2 mg C m-2 d-1 for fens, and 39.5 mg C m-2 d-1 for rice systems. Even lower-emission FLUXNET classes such as bogs, drained systems, uplands, wet tundra, and salt marshes had means of 28.8, 17.3, 11.0, 10.4, and 7.95 mg C m-2 d-1, respectively. Thus, the NEON consistent-source class median was well below all FLUXNET ecosystem-class means included in the comparison.

By contrast, NEON sink and weak-source magnitudes overlapped more closely with soil-scale uptake benchmarks. MeMo process-model uptake estimates ranged from approximately -1.24 mg C m-2 d-1 for tropical deciduous forest to -0.216 mg C m-2 d-1 for polar desert/rock/ice. Soil-chamber uptake benchmarks ranged from -1.5 mg C m-2 d-1 for temperate/subarctic forest chambers to -0.113 mg C m-2 d-1 for cropland chambers, with grassland and desert chamber values centered near -0.548 and -0.435 mg C m-2 d-1. The NEON consistent-sink class median of -0.441 mg C m-2 d-1 fell within this soil uptake benchmark range.

Overall, the ERA5-gapfilled NEON gradient budgets suggest that many sites were net annual CH4 sources, but their daily-equivalent magnitudes were small compared with published wetland and aquatic ecosystem-scale FLUXNET-CH4 emissions. NEON source-site medians were closer to weak upland exchange rates than to canonical wetland emission rates, while NEON sink-site medians were comparable to process-model and chamber-based estimates of upland soil CH4 uptake. Because the NEON annual budgets were substantially model gapfilled, these comparisons should be interpreted as a scale and magnitude benchmark for the ERA5 annual-budget product rather than as direct observed annual flux measurements.
